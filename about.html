ArkiChat
About this project

A friendly front for Ollama.

Ollama is the easiest way to run an AI model on your own computer. Its built-in web page was made for checking that a model is running — not for actually using it. Arki Chat is the other front door: the same local models, the same machine, but a companion instead of a dashboard.

Runs entirely on your machine No API keys No build step Zero dependencies

What it does

💬Chats with your modelsStreaming replies from any chat model you have installed, with a model picker and a toggle for deep reasoning.
👀Sees imagesDrop, paste or attach up to four pictures and ask about them on vision-capable models.
🎵Plays music while you chatAsk for lofi or a playlist and it plays in a sidebar player without interrupting the conversation.
🚀Opens things for you“Open my Gmail” and it opens Gmail. Every action it takes is listed in the sidebar so nothing is hidden.
🎤Listens for “Arki”A wake word pauses the music or runs a request hands-free, so you don't have to touch the keyboard.
🟦Has a faceArki blinks, breathes, follows your cursor, gets annoyed when poked and dizzy when you insist.

How to use it

There is nothing to install. If Ollama is running, Arki runs.

  1. Make sure Ollama is running — ollama serve, or just open the Ollama app.
  2. Pull a model you like: ollama pull qwen3.5:2b
  3. Start Arki: node server.js
  4. Open http://localhost:5177
Ollama listens on 127.0.0.1:11434 by default and Arki expects that. If yours runs somewhere else, set OLLAMA_HOST before starting.

Your privacy, honestly

Arki is built so that your conversations stay on your computer. That claim deserves precision, so here is exactly what happens.

Your messagesGo from your browser to your own machine, to Ollama, and nowhere else.
StorageNone. Your conversation is kept in memory in the tab and is gone when you close it.
Server logsTwo startup lines. Requests and messages are never logged.
Accounts & telemetryNone. There is no sign-in and nothing is measured.
Two honest exceptions. If you turn on the 🎤 wake word, Chrome and Safari do speech recognition in the cloud — in Chrome that means your microphone audio is sent to Google for the length of the listening session. That is the browser, not Arki. And the music player is a YouTube or Spotify iframe, which is a third party that sets its own cookies; Arki never sends your prompts to them. Chat, tools and music selection all stay local.

Who made it

Maintainer

Arki's squircle avatar engine — the superellipse body, eyes that track your cursor, blinking, and the reactions when you poke it — is inspired by Mochi, the character from Coucou by Louis Raillé. Coucou is MIT licensed, and Arki's engine is a reimplementation of those ideas for the browser. Everything else here — the layout, the tools, the player, the wake word and the logo — is original to this project.
